## 2. Hardware Architecture & Power Systems
The Quanser Qube Servo 3 is a compact, high-performance experimental platform utilized for servo control and inverted pendulum experiments. Key structural characteristics include:
* **Direct Drive Mechanism:** The system does not rely on physical gear trains, belts, pulleys, or chains. Instead, payloads (such as an inertia disk or rotary pendulum) map directly onto the motor shaft.
* **Actuation:** Driven by a $24\text{V}$ direct-drive brushless/brushed DC motor.
* **Power Amplification:** Powered via a Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) amplifier. The amplifier rapidly switches voltage on and off to efficiently deliver and regulate electrical power to the motor load.
* **Add-On Modules:** Modular attachments like an inertia disk and a rotary pendulum can be easily attached or detached using magnetic module connectors.

## 3. Fundamental Control Terminology
The notes define five foundational building blocks of any closed-loop control system:
* **Encoder:** A sensor attached to the motor shaft to measure angular position.
* **Command:** The desired target instruction or reference input.
* **Actuator:** The physical hardware that converts control power into mechanical motion.
* **System Response:** The real-world physical output of the system.
* **Feedback:** Measured data sent back to the controller to check and correct output errors.

---

## 4. Sensors, Quadrature Encoding & Data Acquisition (DAQ)
* **Single-Ended Rotary Encoders:** Output electrical signals using a single wire for each data channel relative to a common ground (unlike differential encoders, they lack complementary negative wires). They measure the angular position of the DC motor and pendulum.
* **Quadrature Decoding:** As the encoder turns, it generates a precise number of equally spaced square wave pulses (Channel A and Channel B, situated $90^\circ$ out of phase). By reading the four output states—Channel A rising, Channel A falling, Channel B rising, and Channel B falling ($4\times$ quadrature decoding)—the DAQ detects the exact resolution (2,048 counts per revolution) and determines the direction of rotation based on which channel leads or lags.
* **Data Streaming:** The raw 24-bit digital count values stream via USB back to the PC at a deterministic time-base set by the software loop.
* **Position Conversion:** Raw counts are converted to radians for the position feedback loop using the formula:
  $$\theta = \text{Counts} \times \left(\frac{2\pi \text{ rad}}{2048 \text{ counts}}\right)$$
* **Physical Wiring/Interaction:** Encoder inputs connect to DAQ Channels 0 and 1, while Analog Output ($A_0$) connects to the power amplifier command to drive the DC motor.

## 5. Electromechanical Physics & Working Mechanism
The end-to-end operation of the Quanser Qube Servo follows a strict physical pipeline:
* **Command Generation:** A target voltage signal ($V_m$) is sent via USB to the PWM amplifier, scaling the command to drive the motor up to its nominal $24\text{V}$ rating.
* **Armature Current & Torque:** The amplified voltage feeds the armature circuit, inducing an electrical current ($I_m$) through the windings, which generates an electromagnetic torque proportional to that current ($T_m \propto I_m$).
* **Back EMF Stabilization:** As the motor spins, it generates a Back Electromotive Force (Back EMF) that opposes the input voltage. This Back EMF is proportional to rotational speed and acts as a natural stabilizing physical feedback loop.
* **Motion Execution:** Net torque overcomes system inertia (such as the inertia disk) along with viscous damping to produce angular acceleration and rotary motion.

## 6. Control System Implementation (PD Position Control)
The experimental Simulink structure implements a Proportional-Derivative (PD) control loop:
* **Error Calculation:** Compares the reference input trajectory $r(t)$ (generated by a smooth signal generator) against the actual measured position $\theta(t)$:
  $$E(t) = r(t) - \theta(t)$$
* **Control Gains:**
  * **Proportional Gain ($K_p = 2.5$):** Multiplies current error. Higher $K_p$ gives a faster reaction to the immediate gap, but setting it too high causes overshooting and oscillation.
  * **Integral Gain ($K_i$):** Reacts to accumulated past errors, summing small errors over time to ensure exact target reach (though high values can cause slow drifting instability).
  * **Derivative Gain ($K_d = 0.05$):** Acts on the rate of change of position ($\dot{\theta} = \frac{d\theta}{dt}$, velocity) rather than raw error differentiation. It acts as a brake or shock absorber to dampen bumps and vibrations.
* **Raw Control Law:**
  $$u_{\text{raw}}(t) = K_p e(t) - K_d \dot{\theta}(t)$$
* **Nonlinear Saturation Function:** Clamps the motor command voltage $u(t)$ between $-10\text{V}$ and $+10\text{V}$ to prevent hardware damage
* **Transfer Function ($G(s)$):** In the Laplace domain, the input-output relationship maps motor voltage $V_m$ to angular position $\theta$ using a second-order motor gain transfer function:
  $$G(s) = \frac{\theta(s)}{V_m(s)} = \frac{K}{s(\tau s + 1)}$$

## 7. Variable Classifications & System Configurations
* **Manipulated Variable:** Motor Voltage ($V_m$).
* **Direct Measured Outputs:** Motor position ($\theta$ or $\theta_m$) tracked in counts via optical encoders; Pendulum angle ($\alpha$).
* **Derived Outputs:** Motor/disk velocity ($\dot{\theta}$) and pendulum velocity ($\dot{\alpha}$) calculated in software via a filtered derivative of encoder position to prevent noise amplification from raw differentiation.
* **Hardware State Feedback:** Motor current measured via a built-in 12-bit current sensor in the amplifier circuit.
* **Disturbances:** External disturbances (e.g., human-applied loads or physical shifts) and internal disturbances (non-linear friction/damping).
* **System Modes:**
  * **Inertia Disk Mode:** Single-Input, Single-Output (SISO) where Input = $V_m$ and Output = $\theta$.
  * **Rotary Pendulum Mode:** Single-Input, Multi-Output (SIMO) where Input = $V_m$ and Outputs = $\theta$ and $\alpha$.
